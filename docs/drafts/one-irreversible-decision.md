Most finance apps for beginners let you try again. Pick the wrong answer, tap back, pick the right one. You finish the lesson, but you never felt what the wrong choice cost.

MarketPlay works the other way. Each scenario starts with a real market event and asks you to choose Defensive, Balanced or Bold. Once you choose, it's done. There is no undo. A six-part result then shows how that choice affected your net worth.

I made that call early, and it shaped most of the product. An undo button would remove the consequence the lesson is meant to teach. If you can take a decision back, you learn to guess. If you can't, you learn to think before you act, which is the habit that matters when the money is real.

The same idea runs through the rest of MarketPlay. The paper-trading desk gives you ₹1,00,000 in simulated money, and it models settlement, not just price. Most simulators skip the boring parts. Those are exactly the parts that surprise people on their first real trade.

The calculators work the same way. I built them myself rather than embedding existing ones, because most of the ones I found were forms. I wanted learners to move a slider and watch the curve change, so the consequence is something you see, not a number you read.

None of this involves real money. There's no brokerage account and no KYC, and nothing is at risk. That's the point: the game is where mistakes should be cheap, so they're not expensive later.
